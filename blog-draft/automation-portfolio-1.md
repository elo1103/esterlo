## 中文版

**標題:** 以決策為核心的產能規劃系統

**副標題:** 將產能問題重新定義為「決策風險」問題

**內容:**

在多次與管理層的週會中，我注意到一個現象。
當排程出現衝突時，所有人的目光都會轉向同一位高階主管。
他會快速掃過幾個數字，沉默幾秒，然後說：
「這個先做，那個延後。」
決策通常很快，也通常是對的。但沒有人能清楚解釋背後的邏輯。
那一刻我意識到，產能規劃表面上看起來是效率問題，實際上是一種結構性的依賴風險。
關鍵生產決策高度集中在少數人的經驗與直覺之中。
這帶來三個長期風險：

- 決策難以複製
- 決策取捨缺乏可視化依據
- 組織難以累積與傳承決策知識

真正的核心問題並不是排程速度。
而是決策透明度與可傳承性。

---

### 第一階段 — 在架構之前，先進行行為探索

一開始，我也曾想直接設計一套完整的排程引擎。
但在第一次實際討論導入方式時，我發現一個更根本的問題：
如果我還不知道管理層真正信任哪些數字，
再完整的架構，也只是另一個報表。
因此，我刻意暫緩系統重寫，
改為先利用現有的第三方 SaaS 專案管理工具，
將公司既有流程暫時搬移進去。
目的不是數位轉型，
而是觀察。
我試圖理解：

- 哪些指標真正影響高層決策
- 使用者信任哪些資訊，而忽略哪些
- 組織對結構化約束的抗拒點在哪裡
- 他們過去嘗試過哪些方法，為何沒有持續

在這個階段，我優先考慮的是學習與驗證，
而不是打造一個看起來完整的系統。
我希望系統能嵌入既有工作流程，
而不是強迫行為改變。

---

### 第二階段 — 將隱性的決策邏輯外顯化

當行為模式逐漸清晰後，
我開始將營運資料轉換為可討論的決策依據。
包括：

- 預估工時與實際工時差異
- 分段負載失衡情況
- 超出產能的工作量訊號
- 管理層如何解讀這些整理後的數據

有一次，當某個專案被質疑為何延後時，我們第一次不是用直覺回答，而是打開負載分布視圖，討論具體數據。
那是一個微小的改變。
但從那次之後，討論開始圍繞數據，而不是個人記憶。
原本存在於少數人腦中的判斷依據，開始變得可回顧、可比較、可討論。
系統逐漸從單純的報表工具，轉變為支撐管理層每週討論的決策基礎數據。

### 第三階段 — 在約束被驗證後，才進入客製化介面開發

只有在多輪實際使用後，確認工作流程瓶頸與資訊盲點確實存在，我才開始開發客製的自動化系統。這確保系統架構是基於已驗證的行為，而不是假設性的需求。
設計不再是「功能列表」，而是回應真實決策摩擦。

---

### 設計哲學

這並不是一個排程工具。
它被設計為：
在固定產能限制下，提升決策品質的結構化環境。
系統的價值，不在於自動化多少操作，而在於讓權衡變得可見。
AI 協助加速了開發與迭代，但真正決定長期價值的，
是決策架構的清晰度，以及約束設計的成熟度。

如果你正在思考如何讓組織決策更穩定，我很樂於交流想法。

## 英文版

**title:** Decision-Centered Capacity Planning System

**subtitle:** Reframing Capacity as a Decision Risk Problem

**content:**

During several weekly leadership meetings, I noticed a pattern.
When scheduling conflicts appeared, everyone would turn to the same senior manager.
He would quickly scan a few numbers, pause for a few seconds, and say:
"Do this first. Delay that one."
The decisions were usually fast, and usually correct.
But no one could clearly explain the reasoning behind them.
That was the moment I realized something important.
Capacity planning may look like an efficiency problem on the surface.
In reality, it is a structural dependency risk.
Critical production decisions were concentrated in the experience and intuition of a small number of individuals.
This creates three long-term risks:

- Decisions are difficult to replicate
- Trade-offs lack visible justification
- The organization cannot accumulate or transfer decision knowledge

The real issue is not scheduling speed.
It is decision transparency and continuity.

---

### Phase 1 — Behavior Discovery Before Architecture

At first, I considered building a full scheduling engine immediately.
However, during early discussions about system adoption, I realized a more fundamental issue:
If I did not yet understand which numbers leadership truly trusted,
even a well-designed system would become just another report.
So I intentionally paused the system rewrite.
Instead, I used an existing third-party SaaS project management tool and temporarily migrated part of the company's workflow into it.
The goal was not digital transformation.
The goal was observation.
I wanted to understand:

- Which metrics actually influenced leadership decisions
- What information users trusted — and what they ignored
- Where resistance to structured constraints appeared
- What had been tried before, and why it did not sustain

During this phase, I prioritized learning and validation over building a complete architecture.
The system needed to embed into existing workflows,
not force behavior change.

---

### Phase 2 — Externalizing Implicit Decision Logic

As behavior patterns became clearer, I began converting operational data into visible decision references.
This included:

- Estimated vs. actual labor hours
- Segment-level workload imbalance
- Capacity overflow signals
- How management interpreted structured data

There was one meeting when a project delay was questioned.
For the first time, instead of answering from memory,
we opened a workload distribution view and discussed the actual numbers.
It was a small shift.
But from that point on, discussions began to center around shared data rather than personal recollection.
Reasoning that previously existed only in a few individuals' minds became reviewable, comparable, and discussable.
The system gradually evolved from a reporting tool into a decision-support foundation for weekly leadership meetings.

![beforeVSafter-1-en](asset/automation/beforeVSafter-1-en.jpg)

---

### Phase 3 — Custom Interface After Constraint Validation

Only after multiple rounds of real usage — and after confirming actual workflow bottlenecks and information blind spots — did I begin developing a custom automation system.
This ensured the architecture was built on validated behavior, not hypothetical requirements.
Design was no longer a list of features.
It became a response to real decision friction.

---

### Design Philosophy

This is not a scheduling tool.
It is designed as a structured environment that improves decision quality under fixed capacity constraints.
The value of the system is not in how many operations it automates,
but in making trade-offs visible.
AI helped accelerate prototyping and iteration.
However, long-term value comes from clarity in decision architecture
and maturity in constraint design.

---

If you are thinking about how to make organizational decision-making more stable and transparent, I would be glad to exchange ideas.
